Why this matters

Compliance teams at crypto firms and banks struggle to reliably and independently reconstruct what they knew, and why they decided, at a past point in time: risk scores, wallet attributions and sanctions hits get overwritten by their vendors’ latest view. Cryptomnia is a neutral decision-memory layer that preserves every compliance-relevant fact as a time-stamped event with its source, with revisions and retractions kept permanently, so institutions can answer regulator lookbacks and audits with an evidence trail instead of a reconstruction.

On-chain data is permanent. The meaning of that data is not. Risk scores change. Entity attributions are revised. Sanctions lists are updated. Audit conclusions are overturned. Most tools only show the current view. Cryptomnia stores every version with its source and timestamp so you can answer the question that actually matters: what was true when the decision was made?

Time-aware risk

Store successive risk scores for an address. Later, retrieve the exact score that was active on the day a transaction was approved. A visual timeline shows how the score evolved by source.

Auditable forgetting

When a source is discredited, mark it forgotten. The removal itself becomes a permanent, queryable record. Nothing is silently overwritten.

Provenance built in

Every assessment carries who, when, and which source. Supporting evidence files can be attached to any observation and retrieved later with the claim.

Automated feed

A scheduled process can continuously write new Observations from external or heuristic sources. Timeline, as-of queries, and forgetting continue to work unchanged.

Built on Pod-OS

Cryptomnia runs on Pod-OS, a time-aware multigraph database. Facts are stored as Event Objects with full spacetime context. Previous versions are never lost. Forgetting is auditable.

A reference Streamlit prototype demonstrates the full flow: ingest Observations, view them on an interactive timeline, run as-of queries, mark claims forgotten, and attach evidence files — all against a live Pod-OS ENM.

Frequently asked questions

What is Cryptomnia?

Cryptomnia is the memory layer for crypto. It preserves every risk score, attribution, and claim as a time-stamped Event Object with full provenance, so you can reconstruct what was recorded when a decision was made.

How does Cryptomnia retrieve the risk score that was active on a past date?

Cryptomnia stores successive risk scores as separate Event Objects. An as-of query returns the score that was valid at the requested timestamp, including its source and provenance. The working prototype also shows this history as an interactive timeline.

What is auditable forgetting in Cryptomnia?

When a source or assessment is discredited, Cryptomnia marks it forgotten. The forget action itself becomes a permanent, queryable Event Object. The original record is never silently overwritten and remains available for audit.

What is Cryptomnia built on?

Cryptomnia runs on Pod-OS, a time-aware multigraph database from PointOfData. Facts are stored as Event Objects with full spacetime context. Previous versions are never lost, and forgetting is itself an auditable event.

Example sequence

A compliance team receives a request to approve a $2.4 million withdrawal.

3 March — Vendor flags the wallet
67 · Chainalysis · mixer exposure
The vendor assessment and supporting report are recorded with source and timestamp.

18 June — Internal review reaches its own assessment
41 · Internal compliance review · cleared
After further investigation the team adopts its own score of 41 and approves the withdrawal. The internal assessment, rationale, and supporting evidence (review notes + transaction investigation) are recorded. The earlier vendor score remains in the historical record.

Months later — Vendor revises its attribution
The original Chainalysis assessment is marked as forgotten because the underlying attribution has changed. Its status, the reason for the change, and the date are recorded. The original assessment is not deleted and remains queryable.
